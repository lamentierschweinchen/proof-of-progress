`#7949` sat at 31 commits for three digests running while this run debated whether it was "waiting on review" or abandoned — it was waiting on review, and it merged into a pre-release the same day: `v2.0.3`, the first tag on the Supernova line since `v2.0.0`, bundling 90 merged PRs including the meta-finality-view and contention-handling work tracked loosely across recent digests. Testnet config followed within the hour. This is the clearest signal yet that the `feat/recon-by-meta` branch is being treated as release-ready, not experimental.

### The nonce-delta gap was mis-framed for four digests — corrected here

Every digest since 2026-08-08 described this as mainnet-config (`#259`) lagging behind mx-chain-go's `#7951`. Checking `feat/recon-by-meta`'s own config directly (rather than only mainnet-config's) shows the real story: `#7951` itself hasn't merged into `feat/recon-by-meta`, so `1000`/`12000` don't exist anywhere upstream yet for mainnet-config to pick up. The practical risk is unchanged — if `#259` merges before `#7951` does, mainnet ships `100`/`6000` — but the mechanism was wrong in prior framing. Correcting rather than re-asserting the same claim a fifth time.

### Mainnet is now the outlier, not testnet

With testnet on `T2.0.3.1` and devnet on `D1.11.11.0`, mainnet-config's two open release PRs (`#259` for v2.0.0.0, and the now-merged `#273` for v1.11.11.0) put mainnet roughly one release-cut behind both lower environments on the Supernova line specifically. Not unusual — mainnet cuts are deliberately slower — but worth tracking whether `#259` merges before or after `v2.0.3`'s fixes get validated on testnet.
